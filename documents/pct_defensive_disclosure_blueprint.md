# Broad PCT Materials Disclosure Blueprint

Prepared 29 September 2026 | Objective: defensive prior art | Decision: option 1

## Decision and practical scope

Use all known elements as the organizing scope for a structured materials disclosure. Excluding an arbitrary synthetic element, or synthetic elements and one natural element, would remove subject matter without solving the central problem: identifying particular materials and teaching enough about them to affect later claims.

The achievable objective is to increase the body of specific, useful technical disclosure available against later patent claims. None of the three options establishes disclosure of all future materials. A broad genus does not automatically anticipate every species. [USPTO MPEP 2131](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2131.html)

This blueprint defines the content, evidence, structure, and publication process for developing a PCT international application. It is intended for the applicant and patent counsel. It is not a filing-ready specification or a legal opinion on any particular patent. The starting position is a broad concept with no documented material recipes, calculations, or experimental results. No applicant-specific material entry currently has an established technical basis.

### The three options

| Option | Effect on the proposed disclosure | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 All known elements | Preserves the full element vocabulary. Each material still requires its own adequate identification and technical support. | Adopt as the organizational scope. |
| 2 Omit one synthetic element | Removes combinations containing that element. It does not supply missing ratios, structures, or preparation methods. | No demonstrated advantage for this objective. |
| 3 Omit synthetic elements and one natural element | Creates further gaps and requires a potentially ambiguous natural versus synthetic classification. | No demonstrated advantage for this objective. |

The comparison is a strategic assessment, not a finding that option 1 meets patentability or disclosure requirements. Include single-element materials and their relevant forms as well as multi-element compositions. Use a dated IUPAC element register, with names, symbols, and atomic numbers; record isotope identity separately when relevant. The register defines the vocabulary, not which combinations can be made. [IUPAC periodic table](https://iupac.org/what-we-do/periodic-table-of-elements/)

An unpublished or unpatented material is not necessarily novel, and lack of a patent found in a search does not establish freedom to operate. Existing public teachings remain useful whether or not associated patents are active. This project will not certify a universe of presently unprotected materials or estimate a percentage of future patents blocked.

## United States legal strategy

### Anticipation under section 102

Assess a later claim feature by feature. Anticipation ordinarily requires every limitation, arranged as claimed, in one qualifying reference. A generic ingredient list may leave the arrangement unspecified. An expressly identified species can anticipate despite a very large list; a broad class does not automatically disclose every species. [USPTO MPEP 2131 and 2131.02](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2131.html)

For an anticipatory disclosure of a material, a skilled person must be able to make it without undue experimentation, considering the knowledge available at the relevant time. Merely naming a substance can be insufficient when making it still requires invention. Actual laboratory production of every entry is not categorically necessary, and proof of efficacy is not required for prior-art enablement. A documented workable method or a justified reliance on established knowledge can matter more than a bare assertion that all combinations are contemplated. [USPTO MPEP 2121 through 2121.02](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2121.html)

**Drafting consequence:** preserve explicit material identities and associated preparation teachings in the application. For each entry, say which later claim features it could support and what remains unaddressed. Do not treat a generation algorithm, a formula range, or a database identifier alone as an adequate disclosure of every output.

### Obviousness under section 103

An obviousness argument addresses the claimed invention as a whole. For a species selected from a broad class, inclusion in that class alone is insufficient. A useful analysis identifies relevant teachings, why a skilled person would select or combine them, and a reasonable expectation of success. Predictability, guidance toward the selection, and evidence such as unexpected results can change the outcome. [USPTO MPEP 2144.08](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2144.html)

A reference that is not itself enabling may still contribute what it teaches to a section 103 analysis. That does not dispense with the need for the prior art as a whole to enable the claimed invention. [Raytheon Technologies v General Electric, Federal Circuit, 2021, opinion pages 10 and 11](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/20-1755.opinion.4-16-2021_1764430.pdf)

**Drafting consequence:** explain technically justified substitutions, processing alternatives, and expected outcomes. Record the evidence and its limits. A direction to try every combination supplies little guidance toward a later successful selection.

### Claims and disclosure serve different functions

Patent references can be relevant for teachings throughout the document, including material outside their claims. A grant is therefore not the measure of the defensive value of a published application. [USPTO MPEP 2123](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2123.html)

Nevertheless, a PCT application must contain at least one claim, and its description must permit a skilled person to carry out the invention. Claims must be clear, concise, and supported. The defensive objective does not remove these requirements. [PCT Article 3](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a3), [Article 5](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a5), [Article 6](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a6)

The proposed application should have an identifiable technical subject and claims grounded in an actual contribution. An undifferentiated claim to every material would include already known subject matter and invite substantial novelty and disclosure objections. Counsel should assess whether a supported invention exists before claim drafting. Literature examples must retain their attribution and must not be presented as the applicant's discoveries.

### Later properties and uses

An additional property label does not necessarily distinguish an otherwise identical old material. Inherency requires that the feature necessarily be present; possibility is insufficient. A method claim to a new use, or a manufacturing claim with additional steps, requires its own analysis. [USPTO MPEP 2112](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2112.html)

The working claim map must therefore separate material identity, manufacturing steps, and use steps. It must not conclude that publication of a composition automatically prevents every later process or use patent involving that composition.

## Materials coverage and evidence

### Evidence classifications

Use the following classifications for individual statements and entries. They are recordkeeping categories, not legal conclusions that a material is enabled or unpatentable.

| Classification | Minimum supporting record | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Established teaching | Identifiable public source with the relevant composition, structure, method, or measurement. Record exactly what the source establishes. | Attribute the teaching and retain the original publication date. Do not transfer support to untested variants. |
| Technically supported proposal | Defined material and preparation route, with an explicit rationale, source support or calculations, assumptions, and unresolved steps. | Label as proposed or calculated. Assess whether remaining work is routine or an open research problem. |
| Unsupported possibility | Element list, desired property, speculative structure, or unspecified preparation without adequate technical support. | Keep in the research inventory. Do not count as an enabled disclosure or present as an accomplished result. |

No new proposal has been established by the project so far. The matrix below specifies what must be collected. Its preparation routes and applications are categories to investigate, not recipes or performance claims.

### Composition and structure matrix

| Material family | Composition information | Structure and state information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metals and alloys | Atomic or mass fractions; balance element; impurities; dopants; tolerances; nominal versus measured composition. | Solid solution or intermetallic identity; phases and fractions; precipitates; grain size; texture; heat-treatment state. |
| Inorganic compounds and ceramics | Stoichiometry; charge balance; substitution sites; vacancies; dopant levels; oxygen content; purity. | Polymorph; crystallographic sites; lattice data where relevant; defects; phase mixtures; porosity; particle size and morphology. |
| Molecular materials | Molecular connectivity; stereochemistry; salt or solvate identity; component ratios; hydration; impurities. | Crystal form or amorphous state; molecular packing; co-crystal components; particle form; chemical versus physical mixture. |
| Polymers | Repeat units; incorporated comonomer fractions versus feed ratios; sequence; end groups; molecular-weight distribution; additives; crosslinker content. | Chain architecture; tacticity; branching; crosslink density; crystallinity; orientation; blend morphology. |
| Composites | Each constituent; mass and volume fractions; reinforcement dimensions; surface treatments; residual constituents. | Interfaces; dispersion; orientation; layer order and thickness; voids; interphase; architecture across relevant length scales. |

These fields are proposed disclosure requirements. A chemical formula or elemental percentage alone does not populate the remaining fields.

### Preparation and evaluation matrix

| Material family | Preparation detail to document | Characterization and intended uses to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metals and alloys | Feedstock; melting or deposition conditions; atmosphere; thermal schedule; cooling rate; deformation; sample geometry. | Composition analysis; diffraction; microscopy; mechanical or corrosion testing under stated conditions. Structural, electrical, or catalytic applications as supported. |
| Inorganic compounds and ceramics | Precursors; quantities; mixing order; solvent; temperature; pressure; dwell; atmosphere; washing; drying; sintering. | Diffraction; chemical analysis; microscopy; density; electrical, thermal, or catalytic tests. Separate phase identification from performance inference. |
| Molecular materials | Reaction or crystallization route; solvent; concentration; stoichiometry; seeding; isolation; purification; storage. | Spectroscopy; diffraction; thermal analysis; purity and stability. State the evidence for any proposed optical, electronic, or other function. |
| Polymers | Monomers; catalyst or initiator; feed profile; conversion; temperature; purification; forming; curing; aging. | Spectroscopy; molecular-weight measurement; thermal and rheological tests; mechanical tests. Record specimen conditioning and method. |
| Composites | Constituent preparation; dispersion; lay-up or deposition; consolidation; cure; thermal history; surface preparation. | Microscopy or tomography; interface and void assessment; directional mechanical, electrical, or thermal tests. Report orientation and geometry. |

### Source use and selection priority

Collect dated primary papers and technical datasets for entries whose identity and preparation can be checked. Separate calculated structures and properties from experimental observations. Database membership or a predicted favorable property is not, by itself, evidence of a workable synthesis.

Evaluate candidate entries in this order: completeness of identity and preparation; support for structural features; evidence for any claimed property; remaining uncertainty; and relevance to plausible later claims. Prioritize entries with explicit methods and identifiable products. Do not prioritize by the number of formula strings generated.

The first illustrative case will use published titanium dioxide material research to demonstrate the record structure. It will remain an attributed existing teaching, not an asserted new invention or evidence that the project has blocked additional subject matter.

## Disclosure entry architecture

Assign a stable identifier such as MAT-0001 to each specific material or tightly defined family. Use the same identifier in the description, tables, drawings, source records, and claim maps. The following is the minimum record design for subsequent drafting.

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and status | Entry identifier, descriptive name, version, family, evidence classification, author or source attribution, and date. |
| Composition | Named constituents; formula or fractions with units and basis; measured versus nominal values; impurities; tolerances; isotope specification if relevant. |
| Structure | Connectivity or crystal information; phase; morphology; dimensions; relevant defects, interfaces, and physical state. State unknowns. |
| Preparation | Reagents and grades; amounts; equipment; order of operations; environmental and thermal conditions; isolation; purification; post-treatment; scale. |
| Confirmation | Measurements that distinguish the intended product from alternatives; methods; conditions; raw-data location or source pinpoint; measurement limitations. |
| Properties and uses | Each measured, calculated, or proposed property; method and conditions; intended use; explanation connecting structure and function. |
| Variations | Each variation actually described; bounds and dependencies; technical reason for substitution or range; evidence and unresolved steps. |
| Failure boundaries | Known unsuccessful conditions, competing phases, instability, unavailable precursors, or steps requiring further research. |
| Source history | Full citation; DOI or stable URL; original publication date; specific pages, figures, or methods; dataset version; retrieval date. |
| Claim map | Illustrative later claim; disclosed limitations and their locations; missing limitations; possible novelty or obviousness theory; supporting rationale. |
| Filing location | Exact description paragraphs, figures, and tables that will contain the relied-on information in the filed document. |

### Rules for enumeration and variation

Use explicit entries for concrete materials. Preserve units and distinguish atomic fraction, mass fraction, mole fraction, and volume fraction. Check normalization and define how impurities and a balance component are handled. Keep molecular isomers and crystal polymorphs separate even when elemental formulas match.

For proposed families, define the sites that may vary and the relationships among variables. Do not assume that every independent range endpoint can occur with every other endpoint. Associate each example with its preparation route and supporting evidence. Mark interpolation or substitution as a proposal unless it is established.

If software generates a list, preserve its version, inputs, constraints, and an explicit readable output where needed. The algorithm documents how a list was produced; it does not establish that the listed materials exist or can be made. Make central technical content available within the filed disclosure rather than relying on an external link that may change or may not be treated as part of the application. WIPO's description guidance calls for a self-contained disclosure. [PCT examination guidelines 4.02 to 4.27](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/ispe/4_02_27)

### MAT LIT 0001 Titanium dioxide nanosheets

**Classification:** established third-party teaching. Xiang and colleagues reported TiO2(B) nanosheets exposing the (010) surface in *Scientific Reports* 3, 1411, published 11 March 2013, DOI 10.1038/srep01411. Their paper describes a solvothermal preparation using titanium tetrachloride, water, and ethylene glycol, with heating at 150 degrees C for six hours, followed by product isolation and washing. X-ray diffraction and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy form part of the characterization. The complete methods and qualifications must be read together. [Xiang et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep01411)

| Record field | Captured teaching or limitation |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Nominal TiO2 phase formula (Ti:O = 1:2), the B polymorph, nanosheet morphology, and reported exposed surface. No exact product-composition or impurity assay is asserted here. |
| Preparation source | The paper's Methods section and associated experimental detail. This summary is a source locator, not a substitute recipe. |
| Product confirmation | Reported diffraction and microscopy; any specific later claim threshold needs a pinpoint comparison with the paper. |
| Attribution | Published work of Xiang and colleagues. No applicant experiment or new inventorship is asserted. |
| Remaining comparison gap | No inference that every titanium oxide phase, morphology, process, or use is thereby disclosed. |

**Use in this blueprint:** the entry shows how one formula can require additional structural and process information. Republishing the established teaching would not make it newly available. A new variant would require its own record, rationale, and evidence classification; it would not inherit the literature example's support automatically.

## PCT application architecture

Refer to the contemplated filing as a PCT international application. WIPO publication is part of an international procedure; national or regional offices determine patent grants. [WIPO national phase guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/national-phase)

### Planned contents

| Component | Content to develop | Present missing input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request | Verified applicant and inventor details, filing entitlement, receiving Office, searching authority, language, and any valid priority claim. | All filing-specific identities, route selections, and priority facts require confirmation. |
| Description | Technical field; attributed background; concrete problem and solution; detailed embodiments; materials records; preparation and characterization; industrial applicability. | A defined technical contribution and supported applicant-specific embodiments. |
| Claims | At least one clear claim supported by the description; dependent claims only for supported limitations or variations. | A technical invention that can responsibly be claimed. No universal claim is drafted here. |
| Drawings | Relevant process diagrams, architecture or structure diagrams, and necessary measurement figures with consistent identifiers. | Underlying technical content; measured figures must come from actual data or attributed sources. |
| Abstract | A concise technical summary, drafted after the invention and embodiments are defined. | The specific technical subject; target 50 to 150 English words. |

The description should follow Rule 5's substantive sequence, including the best mode contemplated and industrial applicability where needed. The abstract should summarize the technical teaching, without speculative applications or assertions about defeating future patents. [PCT Rule 5](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/rules/r5), [Rule 8](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/rules/r8)

Organize any materials annex as numbered content within the description and published application. Verify permitted file formats and what will actually appear in the publication. Do not assume an uploaded spreadsheet, external database, or linked code repository becomes part of the patent disclosure.

### Unity and the international search

A broad materials taxonomy does not establish unity of invention. Multiple claimed inventions need the technical relationship required by the common inventive concept standard. Placing unrelated materials into alternatives within one claim does not evade that inquiry. [PCT Rule 13](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/rules/r13)

Lack of unity can lead to additional search fees. The International Searching Authority searches the invention first mentioned in the claims and additional inventions for which the requested fees are paid. Deficiencies severe enough to prevent a meaningful search can lead to a declaration that no report will be established, wholly or for affected claims. [PCT Article 17](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a17)

A no-search declaration does not itself necessarily stop publication; the publication rules provide for the declaration to accompany the published application. Publication with a negative opinion or no-search declaration does not establish the adequacy of the underlying material disclosures. [PCT Rule 48](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/rules/r48)

**Application decision:** identify one supported technical contribution before assembling the first application. Keep the broad index as context and organization. Document any unrelated contributions separately for counsel's unity assessment. Do not automatically multiply applications or pay additional searches merely to increase the number of indexed combinations.

### Evidence and new content

Distinguish measured examples from proposed examples throughout. U.S. enablement of the applicant's own claims concerns their full scope and the experimentation required; it is different from whether a particular embodiment can serve as prior art against a narrower later claim. [USPTO MPEP 2164](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2164.html)

Freeze the complete text and supporting records at filing. Record when each later feature or method first appears. Missing technical information cannot simply be added to the original application while assuming the original date applies; new-matter and written-description rules must be assessed. PCT amendments must not extend beyond the filed disclosure. [USPTO MPEP 2163](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2163.html), [PCT Article 19](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a19), [Article 34](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a34)

## Publication timing and costs

### Two dates with different roles

Let T0 mean the actual PCT filing date and Tpub the actual international publication date. No earlier priority application is assumed.

| Route | Relevant date | Conditions and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public disclosure under AIA section 102(a)(1) | Tpub for material first made public in the international publication. | Public availability must precede the later claim's effective filing date, subject to applicable exceptions. Filing alone does not create public accessibility. |
| Qualifying patent document under AIA section 102(a)(2) | Potentially T0, upon qualifying publication. | U.S. designation; different inventive entity; T0 precedes the later claim's effective filing date; no applicable exception. English publication and U.S. national-stage entry are unnecessary under the AIA. |
| An asserted earlier reference date under section 102(d) | The qualifying earlier date for the particular subject matter. | Verify priority or benefit formalities and the earlier description. This differs from entitlement of the application's own claims to priority. |

Sources: [USPTO MPEP 2152](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2152.html) and [2154](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2154.html). An earlier description under section 102(d) need not independently satisfy section 112(a) enablement; the anticipatory teaching still requires enablement.

Exceptions can include certain inventor-originated disclosures and, for section 102(a)(2), specified common-ownership or assignment circumstances. The U.S. grace provisions are not a general one-year exemption from independent third-party disclosures. [USPTO MPEP 2153](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html), [35 USC 102 in the USPTO patent-law appendix](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html)

**Illustrative timeline:** file at T0; another inventor's relevant claim has an effective filing date in month three; publish in month six. Publication is too late for section 102(a)(1) against that claim, but section 102(a)(2) may reach T0 if its conditions hold. Early publication does not backdate public accessibility. A feature first introduced in month four is not thereby disclosed at T0.

### Recommended publication workflow

1. Complete the technical disclosure and resolve the filing prerequisites below. Submit the application through the confirmed receiving Office and preserve the exact filed version and receipt.
2. Attend to fees, formal corrections, and notices. Unpaid prescribed fees or uncorrected defects can cause deemed withdrawal. [PCT Article 14](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a14)
3. After checking the filed text and bibliographic information, request early publication directly from the International Bureau, using the ePCT Request for Early Publication action or a signed request. WIPO's guidance describes these methods; publication is not instantaneous. [WIPO practical advice on early publication](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/newslett/practical_advice/pa_102022)
4. Pay the special early-publication fee if neither the international search report nor the Article 17 declaration is available for publication with the application. The IB fee annex valid 17 September 2026 lists CHF 200 for this condition. Recheck the fee at the time of the request. [IB fee annex](https://pctlegal.wipo.int/eGuide/view-doc.xhtml?doc-code=IB&doc-lang=EN), [Rule 48.4](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/rules/r48)
5. Preserve the published WO document and verify its actual publication date, complete description, drawings, and intended entries. Record discrepancies and obtain corrections through the appropriate procedure.

Without an early-publication request, ordinary publication occurs promptly after 18 months from the priority date. If there is no priority claim, that timetable runs from the international filing date. Withdrawal, including deemed withdrawal, before completion of publication preparations can prevent publication. [PCT Article 21](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a21), [Article 2(xi)](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a2)

For this publication-only objective, entry into national phases is not an automatic next expenditure. Review the consequences and jurisdiction-specific deadlines before foregoing patent rights. Non-entry generally ends the application's national effect in the relevant jurisdiction, but it does not erase a publication already made. [PCT Article 24](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a24), [WIPO publication guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/newslett/practical_advice/pa_102022)

### Budget and filing prerequisites

Budget for the receiving Office transmittal fee, international filing fee and applicable page charges, international search fee, conditional early-publication fee, possible additional search fees, and technical drafting or translation. A total depends on the receiving Office, searching authority, currency, reductions, document length, and services selected; no total is asserted here. [WIPO PCT fees](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/fees/index)

Before selecting the filing route, verify applicant identity and nationality or residence, inventor identities and contributions, entitlement, any previous applications, and the location where the invention was made. PCT entitlement generally depends on nationality or residence in a Contracting State. [PCT Article 9](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a9)

If an invention was made in the United States, assess foreign-filing and transmission requirements before sending technical material abroad, including through an applicable ePCT workflow. The user's current location does not establish clearance. [USPTO MPEP 1832](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s1832.html)

Other countries apply their own substantive patentability and prior-art rules. A U.S. earlier-filed-document analysis is not a worldwide result. [PCT Article 27](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/articles/a27)

## Tests against illustrative later claims

These are analytical tests, not actual patent searches or conclusions about issued rights. Assume a qualifying date and no applicable exception unless the test expressly examines timing. M-001 below denotes a hypothetical complete material record; it does not assert that an applicant invention exists.

| Test | Disclosed features | Missing features or issue | Conditional assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identical M-001 material | Exact identity, composition, phase, and a workable preparation route. | Confirm that every later limitation is actually present. | A section 102 case is plausible if the assumed complete teaching is established. |
| Same elements with a different ratio or polymorph | Shared constituent identities. | Claimed proportions or structure. | Shared elements do not resolve novelty. An obviousness argument needs evidence for the particular variation. |
| One material in a very large catalog | Case A explicitly identifies an enabled material. Case B only offers unresolved selections or a generator. | In B, the specific identity, arrangement, or preparation may be missing. | List size alone defeats neither case. A and B require different disclosure analyses. |
| Later manufacturing method | The resulting product. | A new claimed sequence, condition, or equipment feature. | The product teaching does not supply missing method steps. Additional prior art and reasoning may support section 103. |
| Newly recognized property | An otherwise identical disclosed material. | Whether it necessarily possesses the later-recited property. | An inherency theory needs evidence of necessity; a merely possible property is insufficient. |
| New method of use | The known starting material. | Claimed use steps, conditions, or application. | A distinct method can remain patentable; merely describing an old method's inevitable result may not distinguish it. |

The identity test applies [MPEP 2152](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2152.html); the large-list distinction follows [MPEP 2131.02](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2131.html). The method and variation tests require a reasoned assessment of the prior art, including expected success, under [MPEP 2143](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2143.html). The property and use tests draw on [MPEP 2112](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2112.html).

### Application to the literature example

For MAT-LIT-0001, the comparison would begin with its particular titanium dioxide polymorph, morphology, preparation, and characterization. A later claim to an unspecified different structure, impurity threshold, processing sequence, or operating condition cannot be rejected merely because it contains titanium and oxygen. A reviewer must locate each asserted matching feature in the original paper or identify the additional evidence and reasoning relied upon. This is an application of the architecture, not a conclusion that a particular later claim is invalid.

### Validation procedure for each future entry

Create a claim comparison with one row per limitation and precise locations for supporting passages. Check identity, units, conditions, and whether the features occur together. Assess the preparation route with relevant skilled-person knowledge. Record missing features and whether the argument relies on express teaching, inherency, or a combination of sources. Check publication and filing dates independently of technical sufficiency. Obtain a materials specialist's review and patent counsel's assessment before using the comparison as a legal position.

Do not count an entry as supported solely because its title, formula, or desired outcome appears in a list. Do not count a favorable search result as a guarantee against later claims. Do not describe unsuccessful, unperformed, or simulated work as measured experimental success.
